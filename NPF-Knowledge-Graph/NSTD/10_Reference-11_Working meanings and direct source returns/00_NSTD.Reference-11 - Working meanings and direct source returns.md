These terms help distinguish questions that occur in narrative work. A local field name does not create a new Core kind. Use the cited definition when the particular claim needs its precision.

| Term | Meaning in this DPF | When a related method is needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| source basis; admitted source basis | Material used for the account: a formal text, event record, story premise, canon, architecture description or another relevant source. “Admitted” adds a receiving-use condition only when one is specified. | Compare ordinary sources directly. Use `G.2` for SoTA synthesis, `A.10` for an evidence claim or `E.17.EFP` for a source-based explanation. |
| selected source structure | The relations or distinctions selected for the narrative. | `A.6.3.NAR` relates the source to the account; `A.22` helps describe structure, `C.2.8` its recovery by a reader, and `C.33` architecture-description adequacy. |
| source-structure selection rationale | Why those relations matter for the reader's use. | [[NSTD/15_01_Choose a Narrative's Purpose and the Source Structures It Must Preserve/00_NSTD.01 - Choose a Narrative's Purpose and the Source Structures It Must Preserve]] revises selection and purpose together. |
| source temporal posture | Whether the account concerns past events, a live situation, a plan, prospective fiction or a combination. | [[NSTD/15_01_Choose a Narrative's Purpose and the Source Structures It Must Preserve/00_NSTD.01 - Choose a Narrative's Purpose and the Source Structures It Must Preserve]] keeps observation, inference and invention distinguishable when their difference matters. |
| rendering mediation mode | Whether the narrative draws directly on source structure, through an architecture description, or both. | Use architecture methods only for the architecture contribution actually involved. |
| architecture-mediated narrativization route | Narrative construction that uses an architecture description, view, decision or account of actual structure as a source. | `C.30`/`C.32` govern the relevant architecture work; `NSTD.1/.2` govern selection and presentation. |
| rendering Work role | The writing, teaching, commentary or other contribution sought in the task. | A precise actual-Work claim separately identifies the System, applicable role assignment and occurrence through `A.13` and `A.15.1`. The task description alone asserts no occurrence. |
| reader or listener role | The receiver's working position and intended use. | `NSTD.1/.6` use it to select material and judge the account; it does not establish actual comprehension. |
| reader-interest or use hypothesis | A tentative account of what the reader wants to understand, remember or do. | A comparison or reader response through `NSTD.5/.6` may change that hypothesis. |
| narrative rendering | The resulting narrative account; where relevant, distinguish it from the construction relation and the file or other publication form. | `A.6.3.NAR` defines the construction; `E.17` addresses publication form. |
| narrative purpose | The intended contribution to the reader's use, including an expressive aim. | [[NSTD/15_01_Choose a Narrative's Purpose and the Source Structures It Must Preserve/00_NSTD.01 - Choose a Narrative's Purpose and the Source Structures It Must Preserve]] relates it to the selected material. |
| ordering rule | The way selected material is presented in sequence. | [[NSTD/16_02_Choose a Narrative Order That Keeps Needed Source Relations Recoverable/00_NSTD.02 - Choose a Narrative Order That Keeps Needed Source Relations Recoverable]] distinguishes presentation order from event time, proof dependency and required work order. |
| event model | A reader's account of events, states and their connections. | [[NSTD/17_03_Repair Missing or Unsupported Narrative Connections (Coherence and Event Models)/00_NSTD.03 - Repair Missing or Unsupported Narrative Connections (Coherence and Event Models)]] repairs those connections; use `C.28` when a causal claim itself requires causal analysis. |
| viewpoint | The position from which the account presents its material. | [[NSTD/18_04_Choose Narrative Voice and Focalization while Keeping Agency Claims Grounded/00_NSTD.04 - Choose Narrative Voice and Focalization while Keeping Agency Claims Grounded]] compares what this reveals and hides. |
| focalized object | The person, object, relation or situation made salient by the chosen viewpoint. | [[NSTD/18_04_Choose Narrative Voice and Focalization while Keeping Agency Claims Grounded/00_NSTD.04 - Choose Narrative Voice and Focalization while Keeping Agency Claims Grounded]] separates salience from an attribution of action or responsibility. |
| voice | How the narrator speaks: stance, distance, confidence and relation to the events. | [[NSTD/18_04_Choose Narrative Voice and Focalization while Keeping Agency Claims Grounded/00_NSTD.04 - Choose Narrative Voice and Focalization while Keeping Agency Claims Grounded]] checks a consequential mismatch between voice and what is known. |
| protagonist | A central participant or focus of the narrative. | Real responsibility or capability, when claimed, requires its own grounds. |
| actant | A function in a narrative relation or plot. | Distinguish it from a real System's local system-role kind or assignment under `A.2`/`A.2.1`. |
| agency; personification | Agency concerns action; personification presents something through humanlike properties. | Use `A.13` for a precise agency claim and [[NSTD/18_04_Choose Narrative Voice and Focalization while Keeping Agency Claims Grounded/00_NSTD.04 - Choose Narrative Voice and Focalization while Keeping Agency Claims Grounded]] to repair misleading attribution. |
| engagement effect | A change in attention, motivation, immersion, memorability or willingness to continue. | `NSTD.5/.6` distinguish these effects from understanding and source fidelity. |
| persuasion boundary | A condition on the intended influence or choice the account supports. | `D.1`–`D.5` help resolve actual value, affected-party or harm conflicts. |
| generated source plan | A plan of selected material, relations and constraints supplied to a generator. | [[NSTD/21_07_Automated Narrativization and Story Planning/00_NSTD.07 - Automated Narrativization and Story Planning]] compares it with the source and draft. `G.2` concerns SoTA synthesis; `C.35` concerns results entering architecture work. |
| plot or event plan | A proposed arrangement of fictional or narrated events. | `NSTD.2/.7` compare the plan and draft; a plan is not evidence that events occurred. |
| schema constraint | A formal or informal rule imposed on generated output. | [[NSTD/21_07_Automated Narrativization and Story Planning/00_NSTD.07 - Automated Narrativization and Story Planning]] distinguishes compliance with that rule from semantic adequacy or successful execution. |
| generation method | The procedure used to produce a draft. | [[NSTD/21_07_Automated Narrativization and Story Planning/00_NSTD.07 - Automated Narrativization and Story Planning]] guides its use; a product can be inspected even when its production history is unknown. |
| repair loop | Repeated changes evaluated against the intended improvement. | `E.22` frames an unresolved improvement question; `E.23` uses results such as [[NSTD/20_06_Declared-Use Narrative Rendering Quality Evaluation/00_NSTD.06 - Declared-Use Narrative Rendering Quality Evaluation]] to choose and reassess changes. |
| learning narrative route | An arrangement of material and learner activities intended to develop a capability. | [[NSTD/22_08_Design a Narrative Learning Route for Reconstruction and Use/00_NSTD.08 - Design a Narrative Learning Route for Reconstruction and Use]] supplies needed support at each use; `E.17` and `E.17.AUD` address the teaching publication. |
| learner reconstruction task | A task asking a learner to recover or use selected relations. | `NSTD.8/.6` distinguish task performance, assistance and the wider learning claim. |
| narrative rendering epiplexity | In [[NSTD/20_06_Declared-Use Narrative Rendering Quality Evaluation/00_NSTD.06 - Declared-Use Narrative Rendering Quality Evaluation]], the reader-relative recovery of selected structure, using `C.2.8`. | Qualify the observer, form, conditions and evidence. A qualitative comparison does not supply a bit estimate, general learning result or architecture-quality result. |
| narrative quality characteristic | One question about a narrative version for a declared use. | `A.19.ECS`, `C.16` and [[NSTD/20_06_Declared-Use Narrative Rendering Quality Evaluation/00_NSTD.06 - Declared-Use Narrative Rendering Quality Evaluation]] distinguish the characteristic, scale, observation and result. |
| narrative language-state facet profile | A description of articulation, closure, anchoring, representation and their relevant thresholds. | Use `C.2.LS` and `C.2.4`–`C.2.7` when one of those distinctions changes the work. |
| pre-articulation narrative cue | An image, tension, mismatch or hint worth retaining before a clear narrative claim or route exists. | `A.16.1` supports exploration without prematurely fixing a route. |
| preconceptual narrative fit signal | Felt rightness or artistic pull before a defined quality comparison. | `C.16.Q` distinguishes this signal from a characteristic value; `A.16.1` can preserve it as a cue. |
| coarsened narrative rendering | A simplified or restricted account that omits distinctions from its source. | `A.6.3.CSC` identifies the consequences for use and needed source returns. |
| explanation-facing narrative rendering | A narrative used to help a reader understand a source. | `E.17.EFP` distinguishes its explanatory relation; `A.6.3.NAR` applies when narrative order is part of the construction. |
| narrative precision restoration | Resolving wording whose ambiguity changes the claim or action. | Use `E.10`, `A.6.P`, `C.16.Q` or `C.2.P` for the specific unresolved meaning. |
| artistic or literary rendering mode | A choice of voice, tone, genre, scene or technique for an expressive or other reader aim. | `NSTD.4/.5` guide the choice; [[NSTD/20_06_Declared-Use Narrative Rendering Quality Evaluation/00_NSTD.06 - Declared-Use Narrative Rendering Quality Evaluation]] assesses it for that aim without treating artistic effect as factual support. |
